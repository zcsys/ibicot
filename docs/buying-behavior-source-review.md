## Identification and candidate implications

An observed average goods price p, requested quantity d and fulfillment f provide
one price/quantity observation plus a censoring diagnostic. They do not identify
both the scale, half-price and elasticity of a sigmoid demand curve. Even fixing
elasticity leaves infinitely many `(L,H)` pairs through that point. An industrial
buyer additionally needs beliefs about output sale price, demand and timing;
consumer surplus is not a derivation of its expected operating return.

The R1 exponential-survival return function is therefore a **game-specific
calibration assumption**, chosen for an interpretable diminishing probability of
selling another output unit over a finite horizon. It is not sourced as an
industry standard. Its distribution, horizon, forecast and cold-start parameters
must face local/market/hybrid comparisons and stress tests. An unsuccessful R1 is
to be reported as a rejection, without modifying demand or guaranteeing margins.
