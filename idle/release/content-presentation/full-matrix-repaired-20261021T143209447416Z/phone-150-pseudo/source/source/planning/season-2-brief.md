## Creative direction

Working title: **The Quiet Current**. After the returning stars, the harbor discovers the slow patterns left in the water. The emotional aim is curiosity and patient restoration: readable currents, listening posts and small lights responding across the bay. Use the existing harbor and producer visual language. Avoid a disaster, competitive ranking or narrative threat that makes a missed day feel like failure.

This is a concept for a later content release. It adds no launch producer, prestige layer, resource, multiplayer system, purchase or asset to v1. No second-season production asset or public commitment is implied by the title. First verify that players understand the existing isolated fleet and archived-pass selection before commissioning variations.

## Rules retained for the proposal

| System | Constraint |
|---|---|
| Main economy | Preserve the five producers, costs, growth 1.12, prestige 0.65, current cross-tier coefficients and earned automation; a season cannot rebalance the main harbor |
| Seasonal fleet | Separate wallet, lifetime and producer state; no imported main Charts, event prestige or exported production power |
| Calendar | Proposed 28-day season, with dates supplied by a reviewed rollout configuration; no active dates in this brief |
| Time and rewards | Preserve 2/8/24-hour main cycles, the 72-hour production cap and six equal free/ad Support slots |
| Pass | Use the reviewed 30-level / 500-XP structure and accessible daily alternatives; no extra mandatory daily chore |
| Ownership | Keep already earned rewards and purchase identities durable; paid ownership requires the configured verified provider path |
| Archive | Preserve the ability to continue the selected archived pass according to its existing rules; switching does not duplicate XP or receipts |
| Monetization | Cosmetic content only; no paid multiplier, catch-up power, timer expansion or automation lock |
| Presentation | Every necessary cue has a non-color/non-audio route, reduced-motion behavior, readable labels and large-text layouts |

The exact future reward IDs, copy, visual set and any seasonal coefficient variant remain proposals requiring their own review. Reuse the current schema as a baseline, not its entitlement/product/reward IDs. Do not copy Season 1 purchase IDs onto a new product or overwrite a frozen Season 1 definition. The current bundled seasonal definition remains only `game/events/content/season.01.json` and its matching bundled copy.

## Evidence to collect before selecting changes

| Question | Required real evidence | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Can a low-check-in player follow the season? | Consented cadence and archive observations, missed-day interviews, actual 28-day lifecycle | Fix clarity or genuine loss before adding objectives; no extra required visits |
| Are Notes understandable? | Campaign goals, optional-spending choices and interviews | Explain the opportunity cost first; do not rebalance from synthetic completion times alone |
| Does archive continuation work? | Real end boundary, delayed return, selected-pass switching, paid restore/refund and offline-reconnect cases | Any lost or duplicated earned/paid content blocks the next rollout |
| Are the choices interesting? | Player explanations of at least two strategies, not only action counts | Prefer understandable tradeoffs; avoid copying a simulated output winner into a required strategy |
| Is the product comfortable? | Accessibility review and reports about pressure, sleep, ads or purchases | Address required-flow barriers and systematic pressure before expanding content |
| Can the service operate safely? | Actual incident/support history, provider reconciliation, restore drills and capacity/alert evidence | Resolve integrity and delivery failures before opening another event |

The corrected v4/supplemental-v2 simulations are bounded engineering inputs. Their 20% multiplier and 24-hour forecast comparisons do not establish an optimal human policy. The optional-Notes ending delays likewise are hypotheses for comprehension/playtesting, not proof of churn or a reason to add paid acceleration. Preserve the current preregistered cohort; a material experience change must receive a separate version/cohort analysis.

## Implementation sequence for the later season

1. Product lead reviews the real Season 1 and launch-support record, prioritizes specific observed defects, and approves a versioned brief. Record what evidence is missing. Do not start a public schedule from a synthetic calendar run.
2. Content/art authors prepare distinct reward identities and finished localized copy/visuals within a separately approved later-release inventory. Run the same schema, economic-effects and accessibility checks. The v1 inventory stays unchanged.
3. Gameplay and platform engineers add the reviewed definition and explicit rollout configuration, preserving immutable old definitions, archived balances, XP and claim receipts. A current client must either understand the new schema or safely keep existing play; never force an older reader to reinterpret a newer save.
4. QA independently exercises upcoming/live/end/archive transitions, late return, timezone/account-day handling, network outage, selected-pass continuation, cloud forks, restore/refund, duplicate callbacks and interrupted migration. Use both accelerated engineering traces and actual calendar evidence with separate labels.
5. Release owner checks the exact build/product/listing configuration and confirms provider identities, support coverage, backup/deletion replay, capacity and optional-feature disable behavior. Public rollout requires the account owner's authorization.
6. During a staged release, pause expansion on save loss, duplicated claims, incorrectly revoked/undelivered paid content, required accessibility failures or unexplained main-economy changes. Preserve receipts and use a compatible forward repair; never restore old data over newer progress as an automatic rollback.

## Required update after launch support

The operational owner adds: actual coverage dates and named responsible people; incidents and repair versions; outstanding support cases; source-linked season/retention findings; content feedback; current provider/store constraints; authorized production budget; proposed schedule; and the next accountable owner. A day-by-day checkmark without real observations is not coverage. If no owner or service is available, the next season stays unscheduled.
