# Creative naming commission: Midbridge Colony

Create a complete, original naming system from scratch for my economic simulation game. This brief supplies the setting, functional requirements, entity IDs and product formulas. You will create every name requested below, except the explicitly fixed title, Gaia, two T0 company names and four main element names. This is a creative commission, not an implementation task. Deliver the full naming catalog, not a small sample followed by an offer to continue.

## The game and its direction

The game is called **Midbridge Colony**, and it begins in **4259**. The colony belongs to a galaxy-wide manufacturing initiative preparing for a coming war. It is a highly advanced robotic society: autonomous companies run extraction, refining and manufacturing, while the human player owns and manages a business within the economy. The game concerns production chains, procurement, machinery, storage, prices, competition and company finances.

The company-number sequence begins with **Tier −1: Gaia, company 0x0**. Preserve the name Gaia. The tier labels are fixed: **T−1 — Gaia; T0 — Extractors; T1 — Refineries; T2 — Manufacturers; T3 — Distributors**. These labels are outside the naming commission: do not rename them or propose alternatives. The operating tiers are:

- **T0 — Extractors:** 20 companies extract **Water, Earth, Fire, and Air**. **All twenty are public companies.** Their existing duties and resource coverage are fixed; this commission only names them. Do not invent, remove or reassign auxiliary duties.
- **T1 — Refineries:** produce 14 intermediary materials: four C-1 basics and ten C-2 compounds. Each material has its own refinery sector, flagship identity and generic company stem. Both basics and compounds use raw inputs bought directly from T0.
- **T2 — Manufacturers:** operate in 14 application sectors, paired one-to-one with the 14 T1 materials. The complete catalog contains 255 T2 products: 40 C-3 and 215 C-4. T2 firms can buy ingredients from other T2 firms as well as T1. Their products may be intermediate inputs or goods sold to Distributors. Name all 255. The naming scope is the entire possible recipe universe. Company population calibration is outside this naming commission.
- **T3 — Distributors:** one million external distributors purchase finished goods. They do not manufacture. Their role label is fixed as **Distributors** and is outside the naming commission. No individual distributor company names are requested.

Preserve the supplied names **Gaia**, **Midbridge Colony**, **Raw Materials Corp.**, and **Elements Inc.**, and the four main elements: **W = Water, E = Earth, F = Fire, A = Air**. The setting year remains **4259**. Other entities are supplied as IDs, formulas or functional descriptions: create their names from scratch. Do not name the initiative, write a new setting paragraph or tagline, or expand this background. It is context only.

Keep it real: use grounded, natural names that people would actually use for companies, materials, products and machinery. Avoid pedantic sci-fi jargon, inflated technical word salads and exotic terminology added merely to sound futuristic. Prefer clear, memorable names; use technical terms where they convey a useful meaning. An advanced setting does not require every name to announce how advanced it is. Be creative in the names themselves, within the supplied identities and formulas.

## Strict scope: names only

You are naming an existing game, not designing or rewriting it. **Do not invent anything other than the requested names.**

- Do not add lore, institutions, programs, historical events, locations, factions, company duties, business roles, supplier companies or other entities. Do not change who supplies machinery or declare existing companies to be extraction-only.
- Do not add or revise recipes, inputs, outputs, sectors, company populations, mechanics, prices, costs, demand, production, storage rules, eligibility rules or company numbering.
- Do not reinterpret the four elements, assign new physical compositions or properties, or invent product-use descriptions and technical justifications for the formulas. A name does not authorize new properties or mechanics.
- Do not add an initiative name, tagline, setting paragraph, fictional explanation or creative-direction essay. Preserve the supplied context without extending it.
- Names, the supplied fixed symbols, stable IDs, unchanged formulas and a factual completeness audit are the deliverables. If an entity or role is not supplied, do not create it to fill a gap. Report a missing identifier rather than inventing an entity.

## Recipe and storage rules you must preserve

- Every listed recipe produces **exactly one output item**. C-1 refining is the foundation: one raw item produces one basic item. Every C-2, C-3 and C-4 recipe consumes exactly two input items. Repeated ingredients are allowed in both T1 and T2. 
- A basic intermediary W/E/F/A has ancestry complexity 1. Each two-letter compound has ancestry complexity 2. A finished product's complexity is the sum of ingredient quantities × ingredient ancestry. It is not the number of distinct ingredient types.
- Example: `1 W + 1 (W+E) → 1 [1 W + 1 (W+E)]` is C-3. `2 (W+E) → 1 [2 (W+E)]` is C-4. The bracketed expression is an unnamed output's structural formula, not multiple output items.
- T1 compounds consume raw inputs directly from T0: for example, `2 raw(W) → 1 (W+W)`. C-3 uses C-1 + C-2. C-4 uses either C-1 + C-3 or C-2 + C-2.
- Parentheses distinguish compounds: `(W+E)` is one intermediary material; `W + E` on the input side means two separate ingredients. Ingredient order does not create a different recipe. Quantities must be retained.
- Storage measures occupied space: raw/C-1 items occupy 1 space; C-2/C-3/C-4 occupy 2/3/4 spaces per item. These reactions conserve occupied goods space, not item count. Machinery also occupies storage.
- Half of storage remaining after machinery goes to finished goods. The other half is allocated to ingredients in proportion to recipe quantity × space per item, so each input supports the same batch count. This is context for naming physical goods; do not change these mechanics.
- These formulas specify the production recipes. Preserve their ingredients and quantities.
- Do not alter, merge, duplicate or discard recipes to fit a preferred name. Different intermediary combinations remain different recipes even when their total raw ancestry is identical.
