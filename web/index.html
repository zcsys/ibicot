Galactic industry · Fleet readiness & civilian life

Tier 1: 10 processed-material cohorts, sold exclusively to Tier 2 companies. Tier 2: 200 invented C-3–C-5 goods and 220 reserved recipes across 10 galactic sectors · 61,950 firms · 1,000,000 external procurement agents. Each Tier 2 firm owns its product routes outright ($1,000 each); its output capacity is pooled across its own routes.
Each firm's output capacity is pooled across its own portfolio: 6 output units per tick by default. The inventory cap includes inputs and outputs; one compound unit occupies one storage unit. Stock targets follow observed orders, including missed orders.

Shared · Pricing & BOT response

Sellers experiment with prices and follow realized gross profit. Variable cost is the break-even minimum; no normal markup target or price ceiling is imposed.

Shared · Supplier reliability & switching

Affects supplier stickiness at both wholesale and retail markets; updates at month close.
Selected company control · Admin

Admin tools control Tier 1 and Tier 2 companies for economy testing. The far-future setting follows a human investor competing with opportunist investors and robotic executives as industry prepares for a major galactic war and sustains civilian life. Future alpha player scope is Tier 1; admin access covers both tiers.

Tier 3 · Galactic procurement

Demand & fulfillment

—

The Robotic Space Generation is humanity's industrial vessel in a far-future society preparing for a major galactic war. Its output supports fleet readiness and civilian life: shelter, nutrition, healthcare equipment, learning hardware, communications, transport and industry. One million external procurement agents represent purchasing needs across that society, rather than a demographic population. They purchase only Tier 2 C-3–C-5 outputs; Tier 1 materials supply manufacturers exclusively. Every market delivers a physical good. Demand-agent count stays fixed, and final purchasing budgets are unlimited in this model.
